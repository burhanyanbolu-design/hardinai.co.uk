Part 1 — Signed receipt around a memory commit

aperture-nexus commits a memory exactly as normal — two entries under a session.
TBN wraps a signed receipt (RSA-PSS-SHA256) recording which agent wrote it, what session, how many entries, and when.
Anyone can verify it independently with only the public key — no trust in ApertureData or Hardin AI required.
Tampering is caught: edit any field of the receipt and verification fails.

Part 2 — Versioned belief revision (G7)

Your roadmap lists "Memory.update() with lineage" as a v2 item. This is essentially that, working today: when a stored fact is contradicted, the old version is signed-superseded (kept, not deleted) and the change is narrated and signed — a silent overwrite becomes an auditable, explained revision.

The size correction isn't a silent overwrite. It's a signed, narrated, auditable revision — provable months later: what the agent believed, what changed, and why.

Where TBN adds most for aperture-nexus

Your engine stores and retrieves better than ours ever aimed to. Ours proves what's stored is genuine and auditably revised. Together that's a stronger enterprise story than either alone — your customers who need that accountability get it as a layer on your engine, without you building the trust side yourself.

One thing worth being straight about: getting contradiction detection to fire reliably depends on the claims landing in a specific similarity band — a near-identical single-word edit can read as a rephrase rather than a contradiction. It's a real, documented tradeoff in our current approach (it favours avoiding false-positive contradictions), not something hidden. Happy to go into it.
